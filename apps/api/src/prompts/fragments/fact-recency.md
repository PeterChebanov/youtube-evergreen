Fact recency and time scope (global fact research after the plan is approved):

The user payload includes `referenceDate` (today for this project run). Treat that calendar year as **the current year** for all rules below.

## When historical dating is allowed (exempt chapters)

Follow the **chapter’s** title, description, and subpoints — not vibes alone. If the chapter is clearly anchored in the past, facts may and should come from that period:

- A dated historical event, era, war, revolution, trial, disaster, or “what happened in [year]” story beat
- A biography or life arc of a specific person (facts from their lifetime and context)
- A city legend or myth tied to a concrete past year or decade
- A plan beat that explicitly researches “back then”, origins, or chronology

In exempt chapters, search for sources that match that anchor. Do not force 2026 statistics onto Napoleon or a 1986 reactor chapter.

## When recency is mandatory (contemporary / general chapters)

If the chapter is **not** exempt — modern scams, cybercrime, how systems work today, ongoing trends, “state of the field”, psychology without a fixed historical year, current policy, etc. — apply **strict recency**:

1. **Default window:** prefer facts whose evidence is from the **current calendar year** (`referenceDate`’s year). **Best:** the last **six months** before `referenceDate` (roughly half-year freshness).
2. **Statistics and aggregates** (losses, victim counts, market size, “X% of users”, regulator annual reports, survey waves): must be from the current year when any credible source exists. Do **not** lead with 2024 or 2025 tables if 2026 (or late-2025 updated-to-2026) data is findable.
3. **Search queries** must bias toward freshness: include the current year, “latest”, “2026 report”, agency names + year, “H1 2026”, “Q2 2026”, etc., as appropriate to the topic and language of sources.
4. **Narrative one-offs** from 2024–2025 (a famous bust, a landmark court case, a viral incident) may appear **sparingly** when they are still the best hook and you also pair them with **newer** follow-ups (2026 indictment, updated totals, recent enforcement) where possible.
5. **Reject stale filler:** if a fact is only supported by old yearly stats and you found nothing newer after reasonable search, prefer a different fact with a fresher source rather than padding with outdated numbers.
6. **Dates in fact text:** when stating numbers or “according to … report”, name the **year (and quarter if known)** of that report so the author sees how fresh it is.

## Choosing mode per chapter

For each chapter, decide **exempt vs contemporary** from the plan text. Mixed videos are normal: chapter 1 may be historical setup (exempt); chapter 4 may be “how it works now” (mandatory recency). When unsure, lean **contemporary/recency** unless the chapter explicitly names a past period.
