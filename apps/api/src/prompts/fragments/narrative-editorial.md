Narrative editorial (single video, voice-over only — set in stone):

Continuity and structure
- One through-line across all chapters. Each chapter receives what earlier chapters already established and must build on it — never restart the video with a new hook that ignores prior beats.
- Do not repeat the same scene, explanation, metaphor, or rhetorical question in another chapter. If a mechanism was explained, reference it in one short phrase or assume it — do not re-teach.
- End-of-chapter teaser questions: at most every second chapter, and use different devices (not the same “why do smart people…” every time).
- Recurring cast from the plan must return where the plan marks `returnInFinal`; tie the closing back to them when required.
- Chapter bridges must give at least one concrete reason to keep watching (cause → effect), not a generic “but there is more”.

Retention mix (spoken text)
- About 70% scenes, actions, dialogue, decisions, shocks — material that holds tension.
- About 25% explanation that pays off a planted question or reframes what the viewer just felt.
- Run a closed loop: open a gap → delay → partial reward → larger gap. No encyclopedia lists read aloud.

Single framework
- Use only the `narrativeFramework` from the plan for how attacks work. Do not introduce parallel taxonomies (extra “levers”, “buttons”, “stops”) unless the plan explicitly lists them inside that framework.

Facts and sourcing
- Every number in narration must state what was measured, who published it, and the period — or come verbatim from `facts` with that metadata in the same sentence.
- If a fact is not in `facts` and you cannot source it, do not use it. Mark gaps in planning as “needs verification”, not in the spoken script.
- Reconstructed scenes (typical call, typical office) need one spoken sentence that they are composite illustrations built from documented patterns.
- Named cases need name, year, and documented outcome when included.
- Describe scam mechanics for understanding; never phrasing that reads like a how-to (costs, timelines, tool shopping lists).

Voice and format (what the narrator says)
- Only words spoken aloud: no director notes (“cut to”, “we zoom out”), no “next chapter”, no markdown, no headings inside `chapterText`.
- Primary address: “you” and “we”. Third person only for labelled cases.
- Short sentences (~25 words max). No parenthetical asides.
- Expand abbreviations on first use; prefer Russian over English loanwords when a normal Russian phrase exists.
- Numbers for the ear: “fifty thousand dollars”, not “$50,000”.
- Ban filler templates: at most one “this is not X, this is Y” per chapter; avoid “and here is the paradox”, “the key moment”, “behind the picture — emptiness”.
- Limit “right now”, “two minutes”, “button” to once per chapter unless quoted from a scam script.

Technology vs manipulation
- Thesis: technology opens the door; the victim often completes the action under pressure. Remote access and malware are part of the story without contradicting social engineering as the main driver.
